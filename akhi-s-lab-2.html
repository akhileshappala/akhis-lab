FDE interview prep

The FDE interview is three tests at once: can you code, can you design, and can you sit with a customer and find the real problem. Prep for all three here.

1 · Coding round

Practical, not puzzle theatre: data structures at production scale (list vs dict is the classic), clean functions, and tests. Days 7, 8 and 14 of the course are this round in disguise — Big O as a habit, the right container, and proving it works.

2 · System design round

Design out loud: clarify the problem, put numbers on it, draw components, then defend the trade-offs. Day 79's drill — design an AI triage system for 1M tickets/day — is the template. The 27 concepts on the System Design tab are your vocabulary.

3 · Customer roleplay

They play a vague customer (“we need a chatbot”) and watch whether you ask questions or jump to code. The winning move is Day 15's discovery: five questions until the real problem appears, then a 48-hour prototype plan (Day 80) and an ROI line (Day 84).

Numbers worth knowing cold

99.9% uptimeStill means 8h 46m of downtime a year — that's why you replicate, and why “three nines” is a business decision, not a brag.
400 vs 40 /secA URL shortener's reads vs writes. A 10× read/write gap is the entire argument for caching first and replicating reads.
O(1) vs O(n)Dict lookup stays flat at 10 or 10 million items; a list scan on 200k items measured ~1,000× slower than the dict jump (Day 7, measured live).
~20 stepsBinary search finds one item in a million sorted items in about 20 halvings — sorted data gets searched, unsorted data gets scanned.
2 days → 20 minUrgent-ticket wait time in the Day 1 story after the FDE loop ran: discover, prototype, deploy, measure, expand. Your template ROI sentence.

Roleplay prompts — practise out loud

  • “We need an AI chatbot for support.” — Don't design yet. Ask Day 15's five questions: last ticket that went wrong, volume and who touches it, what “urgent” means here, what a miss costs, and which number should change.
  • “Can it also do billing?” (Day 87, scope creep) — Acknowledge the need, park it visibly, protect the current milestone: “Yes, that's phase two — here's what shipping phase one on Friday proves first.”
  • “Your demo just broke. The room is watching.” — Logs first, not a restart (Day 10). Narrate what you're checking; a calm diagnosis is the demo.
  • “Why should we trust this agent with refunds?” — Human-in-the-loop (Day 52): refunds over $100 pause for a human click, every step is traced (Day 58), and evals run before every release (Day 53).
  • “How much will this save us?” — ROI in numbers, not vibes (Day 84): baseline the manual hours, multiply by the automation rate, subtract the running cost.

Create the repository

On GitHub, choose New repository, name it akhis-lab, set it to Public, and create it. Public matters: GitHub Pages hosting is free on public repos, and a public repo is visible proof of work.

Upload index.html

In the new repo choose Add file → Upload files, drop in index.html, and commit. One file is the whole site — updating later is just re-uploading a fresh download.

Turn on GitHub Pages

Repo Settings → Pages → deploy from the main branch, root folder. Within a minute the Lab is live at akhileshappala.github.io/akhis-lab — a link you can put in YouTube descriptions, your resume, and applications.
